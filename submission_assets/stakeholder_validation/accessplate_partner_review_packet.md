# AccessPlate Partner Review Packet

Use this packet for short review sessions with pantry leaders, community health workers, safety-net clinic staff, faculty, or benefits-adjacent service providers.

## Review goal

Collect fast, concrete feedback on whether AccessPlate feels credible for low-resource U.S. food access decisions.

## Best stakeholder types

- food pantry operators
- community health workers
- safety-net clinic staff
- public-health or nutrition faculty
- SNAP/WIC-adjacent service providers

## Session structure

### 1. Problem framing

Say:

- "We are testing whether this app feels like a realistic food-access decision tool for people with limited money, transportation, and cooking options."

### 2. Show one or two scenarios

Ask the stakeholder to react to:

- first stop choice
- buy-order logic
- pantry-use logic
- benefits wording
- plain-language clarity

### 3. Ask for realism scores

Use `1 to 5` for each:

- transportation realism
- pantry realism
- SNAP/WIC realism
- source realism
- plain-language clarity
- overall usefulness

### 4. Ask for missing pieces

- "What would make this more believable for the people you serve?"
- "What would make this less likely to fail in the real world?"

## What to capture

- which parts felt realistic
- which parts felt unrealistic
- one or two quotable lines if permission allows
- whether the stakeholder would review again
- whether the stakeholder might support a letter or pilot conversation

## High-value questions by stakeholder type

### Pantry operator

- Does the pantry logic feel realistic?
- Are the pantry/restock assumptions believable?
- Is the source-trip guidance honest about pantry uncertainty?

### Clinic or community health worker

- Would this help patients make safer food choices under real constraints?
- Is the chronic-disease or emergency framing appropriate?

### Benefits-adjacent provider

- Is the SNAP/WIC wording careful enough?
- Where are the biggest remaining risk areas?

## Good outputs from this packet

- concrete product changes
- realism quotes for the submission narrative
- candidates for support letters
- guidance on what not to claim

## Caution

Stakeholder approval is not the same as user validation. Keep those evidence streams separate in the submission package.
