# Support Letter Request Template

Use this as the starting point for outreach to pantry leaders, clinics, community organizations, public-health faculty, or benefits-adjacent partners.

## Short outreach email

Subject: Request for a brief support letter for AccessPlate, a low-resource food-access decision tool

Hello [Name],

I am part of the team developing AccessPlate, an offline-first, explainable food-access decision-support prototype for low-resource U.S. settings. The tool is designed to help users answer a practical question: what is the safest, cheapest, actually obtainable meal or basket they can get today from places they can realistically reach with the benefits, pantry food, transportation, and cooking setup they actually have.

We are preparing a submission for the NIH NIBIB DEBUT 2026 challenge, with particular relevance to the NIMHD low-resource setting prize. We are reaching out because your organization has direct experience with the communities and constraints this prototype is meant to address.

Would you be willing to provide a short support letter confirming any of the following:

- the food-access problem AccessPlate targets is real and important
- transportation, pantry supply, and source-type realism matter in this setting
- plain-language or bilingual decision support is valuable
- an explainable, local-first tool like this could be relevant to the populations you serve

If helpful, we can send:

- a short one-page overview
- a few demo scenarios
- draft language to edit or reject as you see fit

Thank you for considering it.

[Name]
[Role / team]
[Email]
[Phone if appropriate]

## Follow-up note if they agree

Thank you. To make this easier, below is a short outline of the points we would find most useful. Please feel free to change the wording entirely so it reflects your real view.

- your organization and the population you serve
- why the food-access problem is meaningful in your setting
- why transportation, pantry state, benefits, or cooking constraints matter
- why a tool that explains where to go first, what to use from home, and what to buy first could be useful
- whether you view the prototype direction as credible or relevant

We do not need a long letter. A short note on letterhead is enough if it reflects your real perspective.

## One-paragraph draft language to offer

Our organization serves individuals and families facing real food-access constraints, including limited transportation, unstable pantry supply, benefit-program dependence, and restricted cooking options. We believe the problem AccessPlate is addressing is significant and relevant to underserved communities. Its focus on practical food-access decision support, including pantry use, reachable food sources, benefit-aware choices, and clear explanations, makes it more applicable to low-resource settings than a generic nutrition-ranking tool.

## What not to ask partners to claim

Do not ask partners to claim:

- that the app has proven clinical outcomes
- that the app has live inventory coverage for all source types
- that the app has been fully validated in the field if it has not

## Best partner types to contact first

- food pantry operators
- safety-net clinic or community health staff
- community organizations serving low-income neighborhoods
- public-health or nutrition faculty with food-access expertise
- SNAP/WIC-adjacent service providers
